Verisk modernization

Turning 20,000 voices into a clear direction for a commercial insurance platform that hadn't been rethought in 14 years.

Lead UX/UI Designer, co-leading experience design
Product
Commercial property insurance platform, used by brokers and internal teams
Team
Product, research, design, engineering, compliance and marketing

Discovery and experience strategy, Sept 2025 to Jan 2026

How the work moved, from research inputs to what I delivered. The product's screens are confidential, so this case study is told through process.

01

The problem

Context

Enterprise insurance software ages in a particular way: it keeps working, while everyone works around it.

The platform supports commercial property insurance for brokers and internal teams. Fourteen years after its last major update, the experience had piled up usability problems and workflow complexity, and people had to work hard to find, understand and act on complex information inside regulation-driven processes.

The goal of this phase was not to repaint screens. It was to understand what people actually needed, then re-architect the experience: a clearer information architecture and simpler workflows that the new platform could be built on.

02

My role

Ownership

I co-led the experience design work, alongside partners in product, research and design.

  • Set direction. I helped establish the design direction for the modernization and kept it tied to what the research said.
  • Facilitated. I ran discussions across product, engineering, compliance and marketing, so decisions were made with the constraints in the room.
  • Evaluated. I reviewed proposed solutions against user needs, feasibility and regulatory requirements.
  • Translated research. My focus was synthesis: turning survey and interview findings into concrete design opportunities, then into structure and flows.
03

Research, then synthesis

Discovery

The research ran at two depths. A survey of more than 20,000 people showed the broad picture of behaviors, needs and pain points. Fifty moderated interviews showed the texture: how individual brokers and internal users actually move through their work, where they get stuck, and what they've learned to distrust. Competitive and heuristic audits of the commercial insurance space added an outside view.

I contributed to that research, and my main job was connecting the numbers to the stories. The themes that came out of it were consistent:

  1. Usability gaps in how people found, read and acted on complex information.
  2. Workflow inefficiencies in complex, regulation-driven processes.
  3. Trust issues that affected adoption by brokers and internal users.
A survey tells you what is broken. Interviews tell you why it matters.
04

From findings to structure

Direction

We turned the themes into prioritized opportunity areas and refined the personas, defining who does what and under which rules. From there the work became structural:

  • Information architecture that organizes complex insurance information around the tasks people are doing, not around the system's internal model.
  • Workflow maps for the most important regulation-driven tasks, showing where steps could be removed, combined or made clearer, and where they had to stay for compliance.
  • Early interactive prototypes to explore the direction and align stakeholders before committing design and engineering time.
05

Where it landed

Outcome

By January 2026 the team had a foundational experience strategy: the information architecture, workflow mapping, UX explorations and early prototypes that set a clearer direction for the product, and a foundation for the design and development work that followed. My engagement concluded with that initial phase and its discovery and design deliverables.

I'm happy to walk through the work in more detail in an interview, using redacted or recreated artifacts.

06

What I took away

Reflection

In a regulated product, the fastest way to simplify is to know exactly which rules are real.

A lot of enterprise complexity is history, not law. Having compliance in the conversation early is what lets a team tell the difference between steps that protect people and steps that are just old.
