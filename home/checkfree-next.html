01

The problem

Context

CheckFree is the bill pay that sits quietly inside bank and credit union apps. People don't choose it. They inherit it, and they judge their bank by it.

The classic experience worked, but it was built around features rather than bills. To pay someone new you first had to know exactly who they were, type in their details, and wait. Due dates, eBills, autopay and payment history lived in different corners, so the simple question "what do I owe this week?" took several screens to answer.

CheckFree Next was the rebuild. It had to serve 100,000+ people, carry any institution's brand without redesign, move existing users off the classic view without breaking their trust, and meet WCAG 2.1 AA from day one.

PRINCIPLE 01

Bills first

Open on what you owe and when, not on a menu of features.

PRINCIPLE 02

Do the typing for them

Find people's billers for them instead of making them search one by one.

PRINCIPLE 03

Say it plainly

Money and credit reports make people nervous. Every consent and warning in plain English.

PRINCIPLE 04

One system, any bank

A component library that takes on any institution's brand and stays accessible.

02

Onboarding, two ways in

Onboarding · 0.0–0.7

New and existing users needed different first minutes. New users get a three-point welcome (convenience, control, confidence) and go straight into finding their bills. Existing users get a short "your bill pay has been refreshed" summary of what changed, then land on a home screen that already has their billers.

Mapping both paths end to end, on desktop and mobile, let us cut every screen that didn't move someone closer to paying a bill. Onboarding completion time dropped by half.

03

Find my bills

Bill discovery · 5.0–5.16

The fastest biller to add is the one you never have to type.

Bill discovery searches two sources, the provider's biller network and, with permission, the person's credit report, and returns the bills they actually pay. The hard part wasn't the search. It was asking someone to share their credit report with their bank's bill pay.

  1. Consent in three plain lines. The report won't include your score, checking it won't affect your score, and the data is only used to find your bills.
  2. Two lanes of results. Billers that can be added in one tap sit under "Quick add"; the ones that need an account number sit under "Add with verification" and expand inline, so nobody leaves the page.
  3. Always reversible. Skip it, remove any result, or turn automatic search off later. More than a dozen designed states cover empty results, cached results and errors.
04

A home that answers "what do I owe?"

Pay Bills · 1.0–1.11

Pay Bills became the home screen. Unpaid bills sit at the top with their total and due dates, every biller has its own Pay button, and the side rail shows balances and the latest payments without leaving the page.

Instead of a separate settings maze, the next helpful step appears on the biller it belongs to: a quiet prompt to turn on autopay or switch to eBills, only where it applies. Multipay is a single toggle, so paying five bills is one review, not five.

After the redesign, engagement rose 75%.

Every biller card answers three questions: who, how much, and by when.
05

Review and pay

Schedule payment · 2.0–2.27

Paying is one focused screen: amount, deliver-by date, account and memo. The button repeats the amount, "Pay $114.00", so the last thing you read is exactly what will happen. Rush delivery shows up inline, only when it's available.

The happy path is short because the edge cases were designed, not left to engineering: paper payments with fees, payments that fall back to paper, unusual amounts, an existing scheduled payment, overnight addresses, going to a biller's own site, and marking a bill as paid. More than two dozen states, each on desktop and mobile.

06

Activity you can trust

Activity · 3.0–3.18

The anxious question in bill pay is always "did my payment go through?" Activity splits every payment into Scheduled, Processing and History, with filters, sorting and print, and a side rail summarizing each autopay. From any payment you can open an inquiry without calling anyone.

07

One system, any bank

Design system

Because CheckFree Next ships inside many institutions' apps, nothing could be designed for a single brand. I built and maintained the Figma component library the product runs on: tokens for color and type that take on each institution's brand, components for biller cards, payment rows, banners and form fields, and a review step before library updates roll out across every flow.

Engineering built from the same components, which cut dev turnaround by 40%. I also led the WCAG 2.1 AA pass across the product: contrast, focus order, labels and error messaging that works with a screen reader.

08

What I took away

Reflection

In financial products, calm is a feature.

The biggest wins didn't come from new features. They came from removing doubt: saying plainly what happens to someone's credit report, repeating the amount on the button, and showing exactly where a payment is. When people trust the screen, they come back to it.
